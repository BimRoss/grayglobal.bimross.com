Selected experience

Programmes behind the experience.

A selection of the sectors and programmes that shaped our view of what complex development and investment actually demands.

NEOM
Giga-development · Saudi Arabia

A 26,500 km² national development zone in northwest Saudi Arabia and the flagship of Vision 2030, spanning a linear city, an industrial port and multiple sub-programmes under Public Investment Fund ownership. The defining advisory question on a programme of this scale is feasibility realism: separating the phases that can be delivered and funded from the aspiration, and holding capital discipline across a decades-long build.

Glencore
Resources & commodities · Global

One of the world's largest diversified natural-resource groups, operating across dozens of commodities in more than thirty countries. Work in this setting turns on portfolio-level resource due diligence, commodity-cycle exposure and the energy-transition and approvals risk now central to any thermal-coal and diversified mining position.

Roy Hill
Iron ore · Pilbara, Western Australia

An integrated iron-ore operation pairing the mine with 344 km of heavy-haul railway and dedicated port facilities at Port Hedland, built as a single financed system at a cost of roughly A$10 billion and backed by a multi-lender project-finance package. The challenge is the one we know best: making mine, rail and port hang together commercially and in delivery sequence, with the financing read tested against it.

Bouygues
Civil & tunnelling · International

A tier-one international civil and tunnelling contractor with a track record on the most demanding underground and infrastructure packages. Engagements alongside a contractor of this calibre centre on owner-side assurance: testing delivery capability, interface risk and the realism of programme and cost on complex civil works.

North East Link
Motorway & tunnels · Melbourne, Australia

Victoria's largest road project, built around roughly 6.5 km of twin tunnels under dense urban Melbourne and delivered through a major public-private partnership. With a budget that moved from about A$16.5 billion at announcement to roughly A$26.1 billion by 2023, it is a case study in mega-PPP cost escalation, tunnelling and approvals risk, and the value of independent oversight.

Melbourne Metro Tunnel
Underground rail · Melbourne, Australia

A 9 km twin rail tunnel with five new underground stations beneath central Melbourne, delivered through partnership consortia and now carrying passengers after opening in late 2025. Deep urban tunnelling, station construction under a live city and long-duration systems integration make it a reference for both delivery complexity and disciplined cost management.

QCLNG
Coal-seam gas to LNG · Gladstone, Australia

The world's first project to convert coal-seam gas into LNG, linking Surat Basin gas fields to a two-train export plant on Curtis Island through roughly 540 km of gathering pipeline. First-of-kind technology, an integrated upstream-to-export chain and a heavy environmental and approvals load make it one of the clearest tests of the planning, approvals and integrated-infrastructure disciplines we advise on.
